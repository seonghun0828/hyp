# UX Intent — Home

## Page: Home / Landing + Entry

---

## User State

The user is likely:

- A founder or solo maker
- Aware they _should_ promote their product
- Stuck due to overthinking
- Slightly skeptical of tools that promise “great marketing”

They are not convinced yet.
They are tired, not excited.

---

## Primary Goal (UI Perspective)

Reduce friction enough for the user to:

→ **Paste a product link and start**

Not to understand everything.
Not to be persuaded.
Just to begin.

---

## Secondary Goals

- Explain what HYP does **without instruction**
- Establish trust through calm clarity
- Avoid triggering “this looks like marketing BS” instincts

---

## What This Page Is NOT

- Not a feature list
- Not a sales pitch
- Not a tutorial
- Not a celebration of AI

Any UI that feels like persuasion is a failure.

---

## Core Mental Shift We Want

From:

> “I need to think about how to promote this”

To:

> “I’ll just drop the link and see”

---

## Primary Action

**Enter product URL and proceed**

There must be exactly ONE primary action above the fold.

---

## UI Tone Guidelines

- Neutral
- Calm
- Matter-of-fact
- Supportive without encouragement language

Avoid:

- Hype
- Emojis
- Exclamation marks
- Claims of guaranteed success

---

## Content Hierarchy Expectations

1. What this does (one sentence)
2. Why it reduces effort (one sentence)
3. What the user should do now (input + CTA)
4. Subtle trust reinforcement (optional)

---

## Interaction Expectations

- Input field should feel central and safe
- CTA should feel like “continue”, not “commit”
- No sense of point-of-no-return

Microcopy should emphasize reversibility:

- Draft
- Try
- Generate
- Edit later

---

## Emotional Guardrails

DO NOT:

- Praise the user for being here
- Celebrate intent
- Use motivational language

ALLOWED:

- Neutral reassurance
- Clear next-step framing
- Subtle progress implication

---

## Success Criteria

The page is successful if:

- The user understands the action within 5 seconds
- The input feels like the natural next step
- Nothing visually competes with the URL input
- The user feels _less_ pressure than when they arrived

---

## Design Implication Summary

- Minimal visual noise
- Strong hierarchy
- Generous spacing
- One clear path forward

This page should feel like:
“A calm desk with one thing on it — your link.”
